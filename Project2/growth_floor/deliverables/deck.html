2 · How it works

The DP holds equity only when one bad day cannot reach the floor: it de-risks near the floor and in high volatility.

  • Near the floor the equity weight falls as the cushion thins.
  • In high volatility it cuts equity even with a large cushion (φ = 1.00 → 0.07 at y = 1.5, normal → crisis σ).
  • Robust to a halved premium: the breach penalty λ is calibrated against μ × 0.5 as well as five models.
  • The floor is discounted at a conservative rate (the 0.1% quantile of cash growth), which covers reinvestment risk.

3 · The floor holds from every start

Worst-case breach stays below 0.95% from calm to the 1987-crash state, at both horizons.

  • Worst case = maximum over the base model, GARCH-t, filtered historical simulation, parameter draws and persistent vol.
  • 50,000 paths per cell on a seed independent of tuning: SE at 2% ≈ 0.06 pp.
  • Gap-aware CPPI: ≤ 1.33%; OBPI: ≤ 1.47%.

5 · Limits and advice

Use it on long, clean histories with daily trading; switch to the CPPI fallback when the premium or the model is in doubt.

Do not rely on it when…
  • crashes beyond the data are plausible (ν = 3: up to 3.45% from extreme starts, T = 1);
  • the premium may be near zero (margin only 0.25 pp in the tightest cell);
  • the asset's diagnostics are flagged, or its history is short or broken;
  • rates may move sharply: the DP holds the rate fixed (15% of T = 2 breaches are rate-driven);
  • daily trading can fail: halts, overnight gaps, real costs.
Our advice
  • Re-estimate at least annually; run the deployment diagnostics before every switch-on.
  • Add a horizon-matched zero-coupon bond: it removes reinvestment risk and the conservative discount.
  • Next: the rate as a DP state, a DP backtest with re-solves, DP validation on every asset.
